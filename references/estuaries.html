Estuaries

Where a river meets the sea. An estuary is a coastal area where freshwater from rivers or streams meets seawater, often creating a mixture called brackish water.

Two kinds of water meet

Rivers carry freshwater toward the coast. Near the sea, incoming tides can bring saltwater into the same area. The amount of salt changes with the tides, the weather, and the flow of the river.

A changing environment

Estuaries connect land and ocean ecosystems. Their shallow waters, channels, and wetlands can provide feeding and shelter areas for many species.

Why they matter

Estuaries can support fisheries and wildlife while also facing challenges from pollution, habitat loss, and changes to freshwater flow.

In the Willow Pier story

The river in Willow Pier returns to the coast. Its meeting point with the sea resembles an estuary, a real landscape feature; the invented river and village are fictional.

Word shelf

Key vocabulary

Brackish water
Water that contains more salt than freshwater but less than seawater.
Tide
The periodic rise and fall of water level, primarily associated with gravity.
Wetland
Land that is regularly saturated or covered by water.
