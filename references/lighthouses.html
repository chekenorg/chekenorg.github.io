Lighthouses

The guiding lights of coastlines. Lighthouses are structures that display navigational lights to help vessels recognize shorelines, hazards, and approaches to ports.

What does a lighthouse do?

A lighthouse provides a recognizable point of light to people navigating on water. Depending on its location, that light may mark a harbor entrance, warn of nearby rocks, or help crews identify a coastline.

How does the light reach far away?

Lighthouse lamps and optical systems can direct or concentrate light so that it can be seen at long distances. Different patterns of flashes can help mariners tell one navigational light from another.

Do sailors still use them?

Many coastal lights remain in service, though electronic navigation technologies have changed how ships plan and monitor their journeys.

In the Willow Pier story

Mara finds an old lantern on Willow Pier. Real lighthouses and beacons also use light to help travelers orient themselves, although the magical lantern belongs only to the story.
